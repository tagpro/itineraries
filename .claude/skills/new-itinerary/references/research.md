# Researching a trip

The bookings give the skeleton — where you sleep, what you drive, when you fly.
Research fills the gaps, and it is the part a traveller cannot check before
they are standing in it. Four or five subagents in one message covers a road
trip: legs and stops, walks, the paid things, beds and vehicle, weather and
light. Adjust for the trip; a city week wants neighbourhoods and opening hours
where a drive wants road surfaces.

## What every brief must demand

Put these in the subagents' instructions, not just in your own head.

- **A named source and its date for every claim.** "About three hours" with no
  source is a guess wearing a number's clothes.
- **Measured or inferred, said out loud.** A drive time from a routing service
  is measured; the same time plus "allow for stops" is inferred.
- **An explicit "could not confirm" list.** The most useful thing a researcher
  returns, and they suppress it unless asked. It becomes the page's last
  section, and it is what stops the page lying by omission.
- **Contradictions reported, not resolved.** When two sources disagree about
  whether a road is sealed, the page says so and tells the traveller to look at
  Street View. That is a better answer than picking one.

## The questions that are easy to miss

Everything about researching a holiday is obvious except these, and each of
them changed the Tassie page:

- **The hire terms decide which roads exist.** Filter every route through them
  before evaluating it, or you will plan around a trailhead the vehicle is not
  insured to reach.
- **Opening hours against the weekday the trip actually arrives.** Half the
  disappointments in travel are a Tuesday closure.
- **Which pass covers which vehicle.** The Tassie trip needed two park passes
  rather than one, because one day enters a second park in a different
  vehicle — and the cheapest-looking option covered a single registration.
- **The date of every price.** A two-year-old figure is worse than an honest
  range.
- **A named alternative for any leg that can close**, and for any activity the
  weather can cancel, reachable from the same bed.

## Attack the draft before a traveller does

When the plan exists, go back over it once looking for these, which is a
re-read rather than another round of research:

- Does each day fit? Add the drives, the stops and the walk, start from the
  real departure time, and see where it lands.
- Every opening hour checked against the arriving weekday, not the source's.
- Claims with no source behind them.
- The single point of failure — the one closure, sell-out or weather that
  breaks the week — and whether the plan says what to do about it.

Fix what that finds; put what cannot be fixed on the page.

## Connectors, when this session has them

`AllTrails` for walk grades, distances and ratings; `Booking.com` or `Trivago`
for stays still to be booked and for attraction hours; `Expedia` for flights.
None are guaranteed — check what is connected and fall back to web search
rather than assuming.
