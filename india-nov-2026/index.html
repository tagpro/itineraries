THE SHAPE OF THE WEEK

Why it runs this way round

One rule — nothing starts before 09:00 — and one priority: the food. That turns out to be the easier trip to build, because the best rooms in both cities keep short, awkward hours, and a day not spent travelling to a monument is a day that can meet them.

  • ✓Your parents skip Delhi entirely. Flying Chandigarh → Bangalore direct saves them a backtrack through Delhi and a night in a hotel — and since the trip ends in Chandigarh anyway, they finish where they started.
  • ✓Both Monday flights land within five minutes. 11:10 and 11:15. You clear baggage together and travel into the city as one group, with the whole afternoon still ahead.
  • ✓Both travel days keep their mornings. Wednesday's flight is 15:25 and Friday's is 14:45, so neither costs you a morning. That is what makes a five-day trip with two city changes feel like more than airports.
  • ✓Thursday is the eating day. The only full day in Mumbai, and the only one Britannia & Co opens for you — it serves lunch, 12:00–16:00, Monday to Saturday. Everything else that day arranges itself around noon.
  • ✓Bangalore is the cheap city and Mumbai is the dear one. Two nights in South Mumbai cost more than everything else you sleep in put together, so that is the one bed decision worth arguing about.

The flights are booked. Nothing else is — and the first two below are jobs for this week, not for India.

1 · BOOK THIS WEEK
Three hotels, and Mumbai is the one that matters
Same two nights, same square mile, and a tenfold spread in price

Two rooms for two nights in South Mumbai ranged from ₹16,958 to ₹179,360 when I searched Booking.com on 22 Sep. That is the same two nights, walking distance apart. Bangalore and Delhi barely move the total by comparison.

The shape of the Mumbai market is the useful finding: the middle is poor value. Hotel Marine Plaza asks ₹23,871 a room and scores 8.2 across 710 reviews; Residency Hotel Fort asks ₹9,882 and scores 8.7 across 2,451. You pay 2.4× for a lower score on a quarter of the evidence. Either stay at Residency Fort or skip the middle entirely for Trident Nariman Point — 9.0 across 4,132 reviews — and nothing in between.

You said value, well-reviewed, so Where you sleep recommends Residency Hotel Fort, and the budget is built on it. Fort is a 15-minute walk from the Gateway, which is where Thursday starts.

Prices are live Booking.com rates for your exact dates and party, read on 22 Sep 2026. Indian hotel rates move inside the last eight weeks — treat them as the shape of the market, not a quote.

4 · THURSDAY
Skipping Elephanta bought you the two best meals in Mumbai
Both were flatly impossible against a ferry day

Britannia & Co serves lunch only, 12:00–16:00, Monday to Saturday, and is closed Sundays. Wednesday you land at 17:25; Friday you leave at 11:15. Thursday was the single sitting available to you all week — and an Elephanta ferry day would have had you mid-harbour at noon. The berry pulao is the reason people rank it the best Parsi room in the country.

Sardar Pav Bhaji had the same problem from the other end. It opens at 11:00 and carries a 30–45 minute queue, so it needs an unhurried evening. Returning from Elephanta at 16:00 and then walking Marine Drive left no room for it.

Both now fit in one day, with the sunset walk between them. Thursday has the shape.

Elephanta is not gone — the research for it is on Plan B, with the ferry times, the 120 steps and the carrying chair, if you change your mind.

5 · MONDAY
MTR is shut on Mondays, and Monday is your first night
One of Bangalore's two great breakfast rooms, closed on the day you land

Mavalli Tiffin Room on Lalbagh Road has been serving since 1924 and is closed Mondays. It also shuts between 11:00 and 12:30 and closes at 21:00, so it is a late-breakfast or early-dinner place rather than somewhere to end a night.

That leaves Tuesday as your only window, and Tuesday puts it there. Monday evening goes to Taaza Thindi instead, which runs a second session 16:30–21:30 — so landing at 11:15 you can still eat the thing Bangalore is actually for, on your first night.

Airport runs — four of them

Four people with luggage, four times. These are the transfers the days are built on:

  • · Mon, Aerocity → Delhi T1, ~06:20. The hotel's own pickup runs the other way; ask at check-in whether they also run drops.
  • · Mon, BLR → Indiranagar, about 40 km. This one is long — see Monday.
  • · Wed, Indiranagar → BLR, leaving 12:00 for a 15:25 flight — 38–42 km, and the run regularly exceeds an hour.

Book cabs through the hotel or an app rather than at the kerb. With two rooms you are likely two cars each time, so agree that before you are standing outside with the bags.
💳

Money, and what still needs cash

UPI runs almost everything now, but it needs an Indian bank account — so on an Australian phone, assume cards at hotels and restaurants and cash everywhere else.

  • · Taaza Thindi, CTR, Vidyarthi Bhavan — counter-service tiffin rooms. Carry notes.
  • · Street food in Mumbai — Elco, Sardar, Aaram, the Chowpatty stalls. All cash.
  • · Muhammad Ali Road and Chowpatty — every stall on both. Small notes.
  • · Autos and older taxis. Uber and Ola take cards in-app.

Budget figures on this page are in rupees. Flight fares were quoted in US dollars and converted at ₹95.6 to the dollar, the rate on 17 Aug 2026 — so treat them as indicative rather than exact.

06:20 · LEAVE AEROCITY
Out of the hotel, over to Delhi T1

IndiGo closes bag drop 45 minutes before departure. This is the one morning the 09:00 rule does not survive — and there is no way around it short of rebooking.

Arrange the car the night before. Both Aerocity hotels on the shortlist run free airport pickup; ask at check-in whether they also do drops, because at 06:20 you do not want to be negotiating.

08:20 → 11:10 · 6E 873
Delhi → Bengaluru, non-stop

Two hours fifty. Your parents are in the air at the same time on 6E 6634 out of Chandigarh, landing five minutes behind you.

~12:00 – 13:15 · THE LONG TRANSFER
Bengaluru airport → Indiranagar

Kempegowda sits about 40 km north of the city and the run into Indiranagar regularly takes over an hour. This is the longest single transfer of the trip and the one most likely to surprise you.

Two rooms means two cars, or one large one. Four adults and four bags do not fit a hatchback. Sort this at the airport taxi desk rather than in an app queue with luggage.
13:15 – 16:00 · CHECK IN, THEN NOTHING
Deliberately empty

Everyone has been up since before six and your parents have flown three hours. The afternoon is left open on purpose — sleep, or walk ten minutes of Indiranagar and come back.

If anyone has energy: Lalbagh is open 06:00–19:00 every day including Mondays, has benches throughout, and runs battery buggies from ₹100 a head — which makes it the one big site your parents can do on a travel day without it costing them anything.

Don't count on Cubbon Park today. Sources split three ways on whether it closes Mondays — some say Monday and the second Tuesday, some say never, one says Sundays. The closure probably belongs to the aquarium and the State Central Library inside it rather than the park, but no primary source could be reached to settle it. It is on Tuesday instead, where the question does not arise.
17:00 – 19:00 · DINNER
Taaza Thindi, Jayanagar 4th Block

The thing Bangalore is actually for, and it runs a second session 16:30–21:30 — so you get it on night one without a dawn start. Masala dosa, idli-vada, chow chow bath with kesari bath, filter coffee. About ₹80 a head for two dishes.

counter servicecashstanding room
MTR is closed today. Mavalli Tiffin Room shuts on Mondays, which is why it sits on Tuesday instead. Do not walk there tonight.
EVENING · OPTIONAL
Toit, if anyone is still going

The Indiranagar microbrewery, walking distance from the hotel. No reservations and the wait after 19:00 is real — which on night one is a reason to skip it rather than queue.

If you're running behind

· Cut Cubbon Park first. It costs nothing to skip and you pass it again on Tuesday.

· Taaza Thindi runs to 21:30, so a late arrival still makes it. It is the last thing to give up, not the first.

· If the transfer from the airport goes badly wrong, eat near the hotel and move Taaza Thindi to Wednesday morning — it opens at 07:00 and Wednesday's flight is not until 15:25.

DAY 2 · TUE 3 NOV 🚗 no flights the only full Bangalore day

Bangalore, all of it

Lalbagh · MTR · Bangalore Palace · Indiranagar

09:30 – 11:30 · STOP 1
Lalbagh Botanical Garden

240 acres, a Victorian glass house, and a rock outcrop that predates almost everything else you will stand on. Open from 06:00, so a 09:30 arrival is unhurried and the light is still good.

flat, wide pathsbenches throughoutbuggies ₹100 a head₹30 entry

Best site of the week for your parents: level, shaded, benches everywhere, toilets and drinking water — and battery-operated buggies from ₹100 per person if 240 acres on foot is too much. Skip the Kempegowda Tower rock, which is a genuine climb. The glass house may or may not be open to walk into — it has its own ticket during flower shows and none falls in November; ask at the gate.

12:40 – 14:00 · LUNCH
MTR — Mavalli Tiffin Room, Lalbagh Road

Serving since 1924, and a two-minute move from Lalbagh's gate. This is the day it is open — it closes Mondays and shuts again 11:00–12:30, so 12:40 is the window.

Order the rava idli, which was invented here during wartime rice rationing, or the unlimited set lunch thali — about ₹250–400 a head. Expect roughly a 30-minute wait, paid in advance for a token. It has a seated waiting area, which almost nothing else on this list does, so the queue is not a problem for your parents. Cards accepted.

closed Mondaysseated, indoors
15:00 – 17:00 · STOP 2
Bangalore Palace

Tudor revival, built by the Wodeyars, and open 10:00–17:30. The audio guide is genuinely good and the interiors are the reason to come.

This is the least accessible thing on the trip. No lift — there was one, now shut to the public — stairs to the upper floor, and a walking circuit with little to sit on. If your parents have had enough by this point in the day, this is the stop to trade away rather than push through.

Entry runs about ₹230–270 a head for Indian nationals and roughly double that for foreign passports; sources disagree by about 17%, so carry more than you expect. The camera fee is separate and steep — around ₹285–300 for a phone. Decide at the gate who is actually photographing rather than paying four times out of habit. The palace also closes without notice for weddings and private events.

17:30 · BACK, OR ONE MORE
Commercial Street, if there is appetite

Textiles, silver and street shopping, and close to the palace. Skip it without regret if the day has been enough — this is filler, not a highlight.

19:30 · DINNER
Toit, Indiranagar

Walking distance from the hotel, which after a full day is most of the argument. No reservations — go at 19:00 rather than 20:30, or eat elsewhere on the same street.

If you're running behind

· Cut Commercial Street. It is the only thing here that buys you nothing.

· Then cut Bangalore Palace, which is the expensive one and the one with stairs. Lalbagh and MTR are the day's spine.

· Never cut MTR. Today is the only day it opens for you all week.

· This is the day that gives way if something slips, because Thursday cannot.

DAY 3 · WED 4 NOV ✈️ 15:25 → 17:25 morning stays yours

Dosa, then Mumbai

Jayanagar · Bengaluru · Mumbai T2 · Fort

09:30 – 11:00 · BREAKFAST
Taaza Thindi again, or CTR for the benne dosa

The 15:25 flight is what buys you this. Taaza Thindi opens at 07:00 and shuts at 12:00 sharp, so a 09:30 arrival is comfortable and an 11:30 one is not — locals treat it as a daily habit and going twice is the correct move.

If you would rather mix it up: Shri Sagar (CTR) in Malleshwaram for the benne masala dosa — butter-drenched, crisp-edged, and the one dish that might beat Taaza Thindi. Open 07:30–12:30, cards accepted, and about 40–60 minutes from Indiranagar each way. On this morning that is most of your margin, so pick one or the other, not both.

11:15 – 12:00 · PACK AND OUT
Back to the hotel, check out

Most Indian hotels check out at noon, which lines up exactly with when you need to be moving. Be back from breakfast by 11:15.

12:00 · LEAVE FOR THE AIRPORT
Indiranagar → BLR, for a 15:25 departure
Noon, and this is the number to argue with rather than the breakfast. Kempegowda is 38–42 km north of Indiranagar; the run takes 50–75 minutes on a good day and Bengaluru is the world's second-most-congested city, averaging 13.9–16.6 km/h at peak (TomTom Traffic Index 2025). BLR's own guidance is to arrive two hours before a domestic departure, and bag drop closes 45–60 minutes out.

Leaving at noon puts you there about 13:15 — roughly two hours clear. An earlier draft of this page said 13:00, which would have arrived with 70–85 minutes in hand and no room for a bad run. Pre-booked sedans start around ₹950 all-in; an app car is ₹700–900 plus ₹130–200 airport toll, more with surge.

15:25 → 17:25 · 6E 5351
Bengaluru → Mumbai, non-stop

Two hours, into Terminal 2. Confirm on the boarding pass that it says BOM and not NMI — Navi Mumbai is a different airport and 90 minutes from where you are sleeping.

~18:00 – 19:30 · INTO TOWN
Mumbai T2 → Fort

Domestic, so no immigration — bags and out by about 18:00. The road run is 60–110 minutes at that hour; a cab company quoting its own Colaba–T2 route says "1 hour to 1 h 50 depending on traffic". Plan on reaching the hotel about 19:30 and treat 19:00 as the good case.

Prepaid taxi from the counter in arrivals is ₹700–900 to Colaba; an app car is similar but can surge to ₹1,100+ at that hour. With four people and bags, the prepaid desk is the calmer option.

If you want Elco Pani Puri at all, tonight is the only sensible night. It is in Bandra, open until 23:30, and broadly on the road from the airport into town — so it costs you a stop rather than a dedicated 40 km round trip. Any other day it is an expedition.
20:15 – 22:00 · THE EVENING
Gateway lit up, the Causeway, then Bademiya

All flat, all within about 1.5 km, and it ends sitting down. The Gateway of India is open around the clock and free, and looks best 20:00–22:30 with the Taj facade lit beside it. Colaba Causeway and the market stalls run to about 21:30.

If anyone wants a drink and a chair rather than a walk, Leopold Cafe is on the Causeway — open since 1871, permanently full of travellers, and about as famous as a café gets. It is a landmark you sit in rather than a great meal; go for the room.

Then Bademiya — seekh kebabs and rolls cooked on the street behind the Taj, 19:00 to 03:00, every day. Exactly right for a night you arrive tired and want to eat without a plan.

You will miss the Marine Drive sunset tonight — it goes around 18:05 and you are still in a car. It moves to Thursday, which works out better anyway.
If you're running behind

· Nothing about this morning is compulsory. If everyone wants a lie-in, take it — the flight is the only fixed point.

· Do not compress the noon departure to buy breakfast time. That is the one number on this day with no slack in it, and Bangalore traffic is the reason.

· Arriving late in Mumbai costs you dinner, not sightseeing. Room service and an early night is a legitimate answer to a travel day.

DAY 4 · THU 5 NOV the eating day five stops, all flat

Irani bakery to Muhammad Ali Road

Fort · Crawford Market · Ballard Estate · Marine Drive · Bhendi Bazaar

Your only full day in Mumbai, and it runs on food from 09:30 to about 21:00. Nothing needs a boat, a climb or an early alarm — and you can sit down at every single stop.

Dropping Elephanta bought you this. Britannia serves lunch only, 12:00–16:00, Monday to Saturday — against a ferry day it was flatly impossible. Muhammad Ali Road needs an unhurried evening, which a 16:00 return from the island would not have left. Both fit now.
09:30 · BREAKFAST
Yazdani Restaurant & Bakery, Fort

An Irani bakery in a creaking wooden-galleried building a few minutes' walk from the hotel — which is most of why it opens the day. Brun maska, the hard-crusted bun split and buttered, dunked in chai. Add the ginger biscuits and an apple pie if it is out.

walkable from Fortproper tablescash

Shared benches, no ceremony, and the tea comes fast. Irani cafés generally shut on Sundays and keep short afternoon hours — Thursday morning is squarely in their best window.

10:45 – 11:45 · WALK IT OFF
Crawford Market

A ten-minute ride north. Built 1869, and still the city's produce market — fruit stacked in pyramids, spice sacks, and Norman Lockwood Kipling's reliefs over the entrance.

This is a look-and-smell stop, not an eating one — lunch is an hour away and you want room for it. Busy, uneven underfoot, and worth keeping to thirty or forty minutes. Rajabai Clock Tower and the university buildings are back toward Fort if you would rather something calmer.

12:00 · LUNCH — THE ONE TO GET RIGHT
Britannia & Co, Ballard Estate

Parsi, in a corner of Wakefield House, serving since 1923. The order is the berry pulao — chicken, mutton or vegetable, under Iranian barberries — with a Parsi omelette and pav, and caramel custard after.

12:00–16:00 onlyclosed Sundays~₹500–800 a head
Arrive at 12:00, not 13:00. The kitchen shuts at 16:00 and it fills. Ballard Estate is about 1.5 km from the Gateway — twenty minutes flat on foot, seven in a cab. This is the only sitting available to you all week.
14:00 – 16:30 · STOP
Back to the hotel, or the museum

You have eaten a berry pulao and there are two more stops tonight. Lie down, or sit in the air conditioning at the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya — the old Prince of Wales Museum, ten minutes away and the best indoor hour in the city.

16:45 · ICE CREAM, THEN THE SEA
K. Rustom & Co, Churchgate

A counter in a wall, since 1953, selling ice-cream sandwiches — a slab pressed between two wafers, handed over in paper. No seating, no fuss, about a minute of your life. Take it and walk.

It is a few minutes from the north end of Marine Drive, which is the point: you eat it on the seawall.

17:10 – 18:30 · THE WALK
Marine Drive, south to north, into the sunset

Sunset is 18:04, civil dusk 18:27 — about 23 minutes of usable light after the sun goes, which is when the Queen's Necklace lights come up along the bay.

The full promenade is 3.6 km and more than your parents need. Walk twenty or thirty minutes of it and stop — there is a continuous sitting parapet the whole length, so you can quit anywhere.

19:15 – 21:00 · DINNER, STANDING UP
Muhammad Ali Road

The best street eating in Mumbai, and it is an evening thing — the grills come out after dark. Do it as a crawl rather than a meal:

  • · Shaan Juice Center — start here. Fresh juices and cold falooda, and somewhere to stand while you work out the lie of the land.
  • · Haji Seekh Corner — seekh kebabs off the coals. This is the main event.
  • · Shabbir's Tawakkal Sweets — finish on malpua and the famous aflatoon. Dense, syrupy, and one portion between two is plenty.
Two honest caveats. This strip is at its most spectacular during Ramadan, which November is not — expect it busy and good rather than the wall of food you may have seen. And it is standing, crowded and uneven: if your parents want a chair, eat here lightly and keep Bademiya in Colaba, open to 03:00, as the sit-down finish on the way home.
THE SWAP, IF YOU WANT ONE
Sardar Pav Bhaji instead of Muhammad Ali Road

Tardeo, since 1966, and the pav bhaji everything else is measured against. Open 11:00–02:00, with a 30–45 minute queue. It is ~3 km inland from the north end of Marine Drive, so finishing the walk and going straight there works well — home by about 20:30.

Sardar if you want one great dish sitting down; Muhammad Ali Road if you want six small ones standing up. You cannot do both in an evening.

Levers, and the Elephanta option if you change your mind

· Britannia at noon is the only hard deadline. 16:00 and the kitchen is shut, and no other day this week opens for you.

· Cut Crawford Market first, then the museum. Both are padding between meals.

· If Britannia is shut or full, Ideal Corner and Jimmy Boy are the Parsi alternatives near Fort — hours unchecked, so ring first.

· Too much walking? Cut the Marine Drive walk to ten minutes and cab straight to dinner. The sunset is the bit worth keeping, not the distance.

· Elephanta is still there. Ferries 09:00 to at least 14:00 from the Gateway, an hour each way, ~₹260 return plus ₹40 entry and a ₹10 village fee, ~120 steps up from the jetty and 7¼ hours door to door. Doing it costs you Britannia, the sunset and the evening crawl — the whole of this day. Plan B has the detail.

DAY 5 · FRI 6 NOV ✈️ 14:45 → 17:15 Dhanteras — busy city

Last morning, then home

Colaba · Mumbai T2 · Chandigarh

09:30 – 11:15 · ONE LAST THING
Colaba Causeway, and the Gateway in daylight

The 14:45 flight gives you a morning, but a shorter one than the clock suggests — see the departure note below. Stay close to the hotel; this is emphatically not the morning to cross the city.

Today is Dhanteras, the opening day of Diwali — the traditional day for buying gold and household goods, with main Diwali on Sunday 8 Nov. Expect the shopping districts, and the roads through them, to be substantially busier than a normal Friday. It makes Colaba Causeway livelier and the airport run slower, in that order of importance.

Options, all within a short walk: Colaba Causeway for last shopping, the Gateway without Thursday's ferry queue, or Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus from across the road — UNESCO-listed, and still a working commuter station.

11:15 · LAST FOOD STOP
Aaram Vada Pav, opposite CST

The classic vada pav, about as quick as food gets, and right by the terminus — so it folds into the morning rather than costing it anything.

cashstanding
11:15 · LEAVE FOR THE AIRPORT
Fort → Mumbai T2, for 14:45
Earlier than feels necessary, and Dhanteras is why. The road run is 60–110 minutes on an ordinary day, and the northbound route passes the worst of the Dhanteras shopping. Leaving at noon could put you at T2 with under an hour in hand. Leave 11:15 and the bad case still works.
The traffic hedge: Metro Line 3. The Aqua Line runs entirely underground from Cuffe Parade to the airport, so Dhanteras cannot touch it. ₹50 a head, trains every 8–10 minutes off-peak, and the T2 station has a dedicated 118 m step-free walk straight into the terminal. Board at CSMT or Hutatma Chowk from Fort. Sources disagree on the end-to-end time — 45 minutes or 69 — so plan on the longer one; from Fort you are a few stops closer than either figure assumes.

A car for Wednesday's arrival and the Metro for Friday's departure is the sensible split: the car handles tired people, bags and the dark; the Metro handles the day when traffic is the real risk.

Plan B

What to do when the thing you planned is shut, cancelled or under water.

THE ONE THAT WOULD HURT

Britannia is shut, full, or you miss the 16:00 kitchen

It serves lunch only, 12:00–16:00, Monday to Saturday, and Thursday is the one sitting your week contains. Wednesday you land at 17:25; Friday you leave at 11:15. There is no recovery inside the trip.

Nearest substitutes, both Parsi and both near Fort: Ideal Corner and Jimmy Boy. I have not checked either one's hours and cannot vouch for them — ring before you walk. Failing that, Sardar Pav Bhaji opens at 11:00 and becomes the day's big meal instead, which frees the evening for Muhammad Ali Road after all.

The cheap insurance is a phone call before you fly, and arriving at noon rather than one o'clock. Thursday has the shape of the day around it.

If you change your mind about Elephanta

It is a genuinely great half-day and the research holds, so here it is rather than deleted. Ferries from the Gateway 09:00 to at least 14:00 — sources split on whether the last outbound is 14:00 or 15:30 — about an hour each way, returns until roughly 17:30, closed Mondays. Deluxe tickets ~₹260 return and the cheap booth has the long queue. Entry ₹40 for Indian passports, ₹600 for foreign, plus a ₹10 village fee.

The climb is the part to plan: about 120 steps from the jetty, wide and not steep by most accounts, lined with stalls you can sit at. The toy train is ₹10 and covers the flat 600 m to the bottom of the steps — none of the climb. A carrying chair waits at the base at ₹1,500–2,000 a person, cash, but nothing confirms carriers will be there on a given weekday.

Budget 7¼ hours door to door from a 09:30 start, and know what it costs: Britannia, the Marine Drive sunset and the evening crawl. That is the whole of Thursday.

Life jackets have been compulsory on all Gateway sailings since the Neel Kamal collision of 18 December 2024, in which fifteen people died. Hot, and not optional.

Bangalore Palace is shut for an event

It closes without notice for weddings and private functions, and it is the one Tuesday item with a real chance of being unavailable. ISKCON Rajajinagar is the swap — free, and the only site on the list with a lift and ramps, which makes it the better choice for your parents anyway. Go before 13:15 or after 16:15; it shuts in between.

Closer to hand and cheaper still: more of Lalbagh, which is 240 acres and you will not have seen most of it on Tuesday morning.

Rain

Bangalore: the retreating north-east monsoon still throws short, heavy late-afternoon showers in early November — roughly one day in four. Put the indoor things late: MTR, the palace interiors, ISKCON. Carry an umbrella.

Mumbai: essentially dry. November is the pleasantest month there, humidity at its annual low of 62–65% and one to two rain days in the whole month. Sources disagree on monthly rainfall by a factor of six, but they agree on the rain-day count, which is the number that matters to you.

Traffic eats a flight

Two of the four airport runs have real exposure. Wednesday in Bangalore: 38–42 km to Kempegowda in the world's second-most-congested city — leave at noon, and if you are late leaving, skip breakfast rather than shaving the transfer.

Friday in Mumbai, on Dhanteras: take Metro Line 3. It is entirely underground, ₹50, and the one option a festival cannot slow down. Board at CSMT or Hutatma Chowk; the airport end has a 118 m step-free walk into T2.
