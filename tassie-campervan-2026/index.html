How this plan got here

One rule — no unsealed roads, because Apollo restricts them and a breach can void the liability cover outright — reshaped the whole trip. Five reviewers went over this route; these are the decisions that survived.

  • ✓The Tasman Peninsula is out entirely. It's a 190 km dead-end spur, the wettest place on the east coast at 13.4 wet days in 30, and it's where the gravel rule bites hardest — it locks out Cape Hauy and Cape Raoul, the two best walks there.
  • ✓Bay of Fires replaces Maria Island. New ground every day, no ferry timetable, and no fare. The Gardens Road is sealed its full 13 km.
  • ✕Cape Tourville is out. Sources conflict, but most put it at 5–7 km of gravel. Not worth the cover.
  • ✕So are Skeleton Bay, Dora's Point, Grants Point and every Bay of Fires beach spur — all unsealed. Peron Dunes is 4WD-only soft sand, rated Difficult by Parks. You walk to the boulders from the sealed road instead.
  • ✓Richardsons Beach is out — its pads are listed for 5.4 m vans and yours is 5.94 m. BIG4 Iluka takes the Freycinet night.

Read this first

Five things a review round changed. The first one moves your whole last day.

1 · VERIFY
The van may be due back at 2:00 pm, not 2:30
Apollo Hobart · this changes Wednesday

Apollo's own Hobart branch page publishes hours as Mon–Sun 9 am – 3:00 pm, not 9:00–3:30. And the "return an hour before closing" rule turns up in Mighty's terms, not in the Apollo AU rental agreement.

The Wednesday below is built to hit the depot at 13:55, which survives either reading. But confirm both the closing time and the buffer against your booking confirmation.

Also confirm the van is handed over full of fuel — Apollo is full-to-full, and it changes whether you need a stop on Saturday.

☎ Apollo 1800 777 779
2 · SAVE $48
Don't buy the Holiday Pass — Freycinet is the only park you pay for
Bay of Fires and kunanyi are both free

Bay of Fires Conservation Area is not a national park — free, including its campgrounds. Wellington Park on kunanyi is free too. Buckland, Orford, Spiky Bridge and the Bicheno Blowhole cost nothing. On this route Freycinet is the only gate you pay at.

So the $98.35 Holiday Pass is roughly double what you need. An Annual One-Park pass for Freycinet is $50.70; a single 24-hour vehicle pass is $46.60 and would cover you if Monday's walk finishes within 24 hours of Sunday's arrival.

⚠ Sources disagree on whether the $50.70 One-Park pass is per vehicle or per adult. One phone call settles it — and it's the difference between saving $48 and saving nothing.

☎ Parks 1300 827 727 Buy online →
3 · SATURDAY
You won't be walking until midday on Day 1
The handover is longer than you'd think

The depot doesn't open until 09:00, Apollo runs no airport shuttle (take a taxi to 14 Long St), and a first motorhome handover runs 60–90 minutes. Add a four-night shop at Sorell and you leave town around 11:00.

Realistic: parked at Orford 11:45–12:15, not 10:30. That's still fine — the walk below is under two hours and sunset is 17:56 — but plan lunch into it rather than racing.

Book a Woolworths Sorell click-and-collect slot for ~10:30 and you get most of that hour back.

4 · MONDAY
Mount Amos is a coin-flip, so it has a real fallback
Dry granite or don't go

Good news: Coles Bay sits in an east-coast rain shadow and September gets only ~46–63 mm over about five rain days, so 70–80% of mornings are rain-free.

The catch is dew. Overnight minima of 5–7 °C leave the slabs wet and the low September sun dries them slowly. Realistically the rock is reliably dry by 09:00 on maybe half of mornings — but nearer 70% by 11:00. AllTrails' recommended season is October to April.

So: 09:00 go/no-go, and start at 10:00, not dawn. If it's wet, don't improvise — drive north early and spend the hours at Bay of Fires instead, where the walking is genuinely good and nothing depends on the weather.

5 · EXPECTATIONS
This trip never sees Salamanca Market
And Bay of Fires is a sunrise, not a sunset

Salamanca Market runs Saturdays only, 08:30–15:00. Your Saturday morning is the depot handover, and Wednesday's Salamanca stop will be shops and galleries. Worth knowing now rather than on the day.

Bay of Fires faces east. The famous shot is sunrise, around 06:11 — which is exactly why the plan gives you Tuesday morning there rather than a rushed afternoon.

Buckland church may be locked. It's decommissioned and volunteer-run with no published hours. Some visitors walk straight in, others find it shut. Don't build Saturday around getting inside.

Two things the forecast won't tell you

Daylight is not a constraint. Mid-September gives just under 12 hours, roughly 06:15 to 18:00 — every walk here fits comfortably. Daylight saving doesn't start until October.

Dusk driving is the real risk. Tasmania has the highest per-capita wildlife road toll in the world, most strikes happen between dusk and dawn, and about half involve vehicles doing over 80 km/h. The Buckland and Orford hills — Bust-Me-Gall and Break-Me-Neck — are notorious. This itinerary is built to get you off the highway before dark every night. Keep it under 80 from 17:00, use high beam where you can, and never swerve.

Pack for wind. September is the windiest stretch of the year and the Hazards saddle, the Bay of Fires coast and the Organ Pipes traverse are all exposed. A real windproof shell, beanie and gloves.

The most-reviewed walk in eastern Tasmania, and the reason people come. Go clockwise: stairs up to the lookout, down to the beach, flat across the isthmus, back along Hazards Beach. The crowds thin the moment you pass the lookout.

09:00 · GO / NO-GO
Decide on Mount Amos over breakfast

Two questions: has it been dry for 24 hours, and is the forecast dry? If either answer is no, don't go. The granite slabs lose all grip with the slightest moisture and there is nothing to hold.

If it's a no: leave Coles Bay at 09:00 instead, reach Bay of Fires by 11:30, and give the whole afternoon to the coast walks below. That's a genuinely good day, not a consolation — which is exactly why this fallback exists.

08:35 – 12:05 · MAIN EVENT
Organ Pipes Circuit, from The Springs
9.5 km loop 3–3½ hrs Grade 4 · 486 m free — no pass ★4.7

Up through the Sphinx Rock lookout, traversing beneath the dolerite columns themselves, round via Junction Cabin and The Chalet. Tops out around 1,014 m — high enough for the weather to be its own thing.

This is 9.5 km, not the 6.4 km you may have seen. That shorter figure belongs to a different track that summits via the Zig Zag at 1,260 m. The Springs circuit is longer, lower and the right one for a departure day.

Boots, not trail runners — the Sphinx Rock to Junction Cabin section is rough and rocky, and wetter in late winter. Poles help. Snow is possible; a "no access above The Springs" alert was live as recently as late August.

Walks the gravel rule cost you

Plan B

Five ways this trip can wobble, and what you do instead. None of them ruin a day if you've decided in advance.

RISK 1

The depot closes at 3:00, not 3:30

Apollo's Hobart page publishes 9 am – 3:00 pm. With the usual one-hour buffer that makes your deadline 14:00.

Already handled: Wednesday is built to reach the depot at 13:55 either way. But confirm it — and if it turns out to be 14:30, you've simply bought yourself 35 minutes at Salamanca.

RISK 2

Wet granite on Mount Amos

Roughly a coin-flip by 09:00 in September, better by 11:00. Dew, not rain, is usually the culprit.

Leave Coles Bay at 09:00 instead, reach Bay of Fires by 11:30, and give the whole afternoon to The Gardens and Binalong Bay. You lose a summit and gain a coast — it's a fair trade, and it's why Tuesday's walk isn't the only one up there.

RISK 3

Pinnacle Road shut for ice

Snow and ice restrict it April–September, and closures come at short notice. The usual gate is at The Springs — which blocks the summit but not your walk, since the circuit starts there. A closure lower down at Fern Tree is the one that hurts.

Swap in Richmond, twelve minutes from the depot: oldest stone bridge in Australia, oldest intact gaol, Coal River Valley cellar doors. Or Bonorong for guaranteed devils. Check the status page the night before and again at 06:00.

RISK 4

BIG4 Iluka is full

Coles Bay has one conventional powered park and you need it on a Sunday. Richardsons Beach is ruled out on van length; everything else nearby is unpowered.

Freycinet Resort at Swanwick has powered ensuite caravan sites, 4–6 km out. Failing that it's Bicheno, 45 minutes north — which would mean driving back for Monday morning. Book early and this never comes up.

RISK 5

Running late on Tuesday

Tuesday is the longest drive of the trip and it ends near dusk. If you're still at Bay of Fires past 14:30, you'll finish the Buckland hills in the dark — the worst wildlife stretch on the route, and animal-strike damage is usually excluded from rental cover.

Set a hard 14:30 departure. If you blow it, stop at Swansea instead and ring Triabunna — better a changed plan than the last hour driven at 100 km/h among wallabies.

What couldn't be confirmed

Five reviewers went over this route. These are the things none of them could settle, so you don't act on a number that was inferred rather than found:

  • The return deadline. Apollo's Hobart page says 9 am–3:00 pm; the brief said 3:30. The "one hour before closing" rule was found in a competitor's terms, not Apollo's. Wednesday is built for 13:55 so it works either way, but confirm it.
  • Whether the $50.70 Freycinet One-Park pass is per vehicle or per adult. Sources split. It's the difference between saving $48 and saving nothing.
  • Cape Tourville's surface. Most sources say 5–7 km of gravel; a minority say sealed. Left out on the precautionary reading.
  • Powered-site rates and site length limits. None of the three parks publishes a maximum vehicle length, and live availability for these dates couldn't be checked. Declare 5.94 m when you book.
  • The Spiky Bridge pull-off and the Devil's Corner driveway. Both reported as dirt, neither authoritatively. Look before you turn in.
  • How often Mount Amos is actually dry. The "about half by 09:00" figure is reasoning from Coles Bay's September climate plus dew, not a published statistic.
  • Live Parks Tasmania track alerts. The alerts register was unreachable from here. Check it the week you travel — and Pinnacle Road on the morning itself.
