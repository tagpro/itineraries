How this plan got here

One rule — no unsealed roads, because Apollo restricts them and a breach can void the liability cover outright — reshaped the whole trip:

  • ✕Mount Field is out. Every alpine walk there starts at the top of Lake Dobson Road — 16 km of gravel Parks calls unsuitable for campervans.
  • ✕Cape Hauy is out (12 km unsealed), and with it Cape Raoul, Waterfall Bay and Friendly Beaches.
  • ✓Crescent Bay replaces Cape Hauy. From the Remarkable Cave car park — the peninsula's one genuine half-day walk with confirmed sealed access.
  • ✓Maria Island replaces Mount Field. It's car-free, so the gravel problem simply doesn't exist there. Tuesday goes from your longest drive to your biggest hiking day.

Read this first

Five things that differ from what you'd assume. The first one decides your Tuesday.

1 · BOOK NOW
Your whole Tuesday hangs on the 8:30 am ferry
Encounter Maria Island · departures are “subject to demand”

Good news first: 15 September falls in the summer timetable (September–April, daily), so the Tuesday exclusion of the winter schedule doesn't apply. The ferry runs.

But the operator says departures are “subject to demand and safe sailing conditions,” and mid-September is shoulder season. If you end up on the 10:00 am instead of the 8:30, your island window drops from ~7 hours to ~5½ — and Bishop and Clerk alone eats five of those. That's the difference between a good day and a rushed one.

Book the 8:30 am the moment 2026 dates open, and phone to confirm it's actually running.

Book the ferry → ☎ 03 6224 8333
2 · TIDE
Painted Cliffs won't work on 15 September — don't plan around it
The tide is against you all day

Parks: the rock platform is “safest within two hours of low tide.” On Tuesday the lows fall at 4:51 am and 5:49 pm — one before the first ferry, one after the last. Your whole island day straddles a high tide at 11:05 am.

The least-bad window is roughly 3:15–4:00 pm, which is exactly when you need to be walking back for the 4:15 ferry. It's a 30-minute walk each way. Not worth the squeeze.

Silver lining: the range here is small — about 0.7 m — so it's less dramatic than it sounds. Tide figures are a third-party prediction for Spring Bay; cross-check the BOM 2026 table if you want to try anyway.

3 · CARRY IT ALL
Maria Island has no shop, no café, and no drinkable water
Plus construction at Darlington all year

The island is completely unserviced. Taps exist at Darlington but all island water requires treatment. The only food or drink you can buy all day is on the ferry itself. Carry 2–3 L each — Bishop and Clerk is a 600 m climb with no shade at the top — plus all your food, and carry your rubbish back out.

Toilets and BBQs at Darlington; showers are out of action. Provision at Swansea or Triabunna the day before.

Active works: water, wastewater and electrical upgrades run at Darlington from Dec 2025 to Nov 2026 — heavy machinery, noise and dust in the township. No walking tracks are closed, but check Parks alerts the week before.

4 · PASS
Bring the pass receipt on the ferry — the van stays behind
Staff check passes at the Gateway

Maria Island is a national park and Encounter staff verify passes at check-in. A vehicle Holiday Pass is normally tied to the van by rego and windscreen display — and on Tuesday the van is sitting in Triabunna while you arrive on foot. Carry the receipt, printed or on your phone.

The maths still works: you enter Tasman NP, Freycinet and Maria Island — three parks. Holiday Pass (vehicle) $98.35 beats three daily passes at $49.15 comfortably. kunanyi is in Wellington Park and is free.

5 · TIMING
Van back by 2:30 pm Wednesday, not 3:00
Depot closes 3:30, return required an hour before

Apollo Hobart (14 Long St, Cambridge) trades 9:00 am – 3:30 pm and wants vehicles back at least an hour before closing. Day 5 is built to 2:30 with a 30-minute buffer.

Also note Tuesday's last ferry lands about 4:45 pm and sunset is 5:57 — so the Triabunna → Cambridge drive finishes at dusk. Allow for it, and watch for wildlife on the Buckland hills.

09:05 – 13:45 · MAIN EVENT
Bishop and Clerk
11 km return 4–5 hrs Grade 4 620 m summit exposed

Straight up from sea level through grassland and forest to a dolerite summit block, with boulder fields and a real scramble at the top. One of Tasmania's 60 Great Short Walks, and the reason to come.

Parks: “the summit is exposed and should be avoided in strong, windy conditions.” High unfenced cliffs and sheer drops. Wet dolerite is slippery, and the final scramble is where the exposure and the fall risk coincide. If it's wet or blowing, take the view from below the boulder field — the summit block adds little for a lot of risk. Decide the night before off the BOM East Coast forecast.
Parks Tasmania →
13:45
Lunch at Darlington, among the wombats

The grasslands around the airstrip are the most reliable wombat viewing in Tasmania, and Cape Barren geese are usually about. Keep 2 m back. Devils are nocturnal — you won't see one on a day trip.

14:15 – 16:00
Fossil Cliffs Circuit, or the convict settlement
4.5 km circuit1–2½ hrsGrade 3

Across the grassy airstrip, then a steep drop to a rock shelf packed with 300-million-year-old shells and corals. It shares its first section with the Bishop and Clerk approach, so it slots in naturally.

If Bishop and Clerk ran to the full five hours, swap this for the Darlington convict settlement instead — UNESCO-listed, penitentiary and Coffee Palace, 30–60 flexible minutes right by the jetty.

Drive to Cambridge · 72 km, sealed

A3 through Orford, over the Bust-Me-Gall and Break-Me-Neck hills via Buckland, then Sorell and the causeways. Winding and hilly but a sealed A-road throughout.

Sunset is 17:57, so the second half is in the dark on a road with a lot of wildlife. Take it slowly — and if you'd rather not, Richmond is a 15-minute detour before Cambridge and makes a good dinner stop with the 1823 bridge lit up.
If the 8:30 ferry doesn't run read this
On the 10:00 am instead — window drops to 5½ hours

Land 10:30, back at the jetty 16:00. Bishop and Clerk alone, and even that is tight at the five-hour end. Drop Fossil Cliffs, walk fast, and turn round at the boulder field if you're behind schedule. Look at the convict buildings while you wait for the boat.

Bikes are an option, but not for the summits

Hire on the island next to the penitentiary, $40/day, until 3:15 pm. But bikes are restricted to roads and vehicle tracks — not walking tracks — so they get you to the approaches, not up Bishop and Clerk. Book 24 hrs ahead with your ferry ticket.

Mount Maria doesn't fit — don't be tempted

16 km, about 8 hours, Grade 4. Your on-island window is roughly 7 hours on the best ferry. Missing the 4:15 means a night on an island with no food, no shop and nowhere booked.

Painted Cliffs — only if you accept the compromise

4.3 km return, 1–2 hrs, Grade 3, and genuinely beautiful. But Tuesday's tide is wrong (see the alert on Overview) and the safe window collides with the last ferry. If the cliffs matter more than the summit, do Painted Cliffs plus Fossil Cliffs and skip Bishop and Clerk — a gentler day, and the tide is less punishing on a 2-hour visit than the platform rules suggest, given the small range here.

Plan B

Four things can go wrong. None of them ruin the trip if you've read this first.

RISK 1

The 8:30 ferry doesn't run

Sailings are “subject to demand”, and mid-September is quiet. On the 10:00 your island window drops from ~7 hours to ~5½.

Then it's Bishop and Clerk alone — drop Fossil Cliffs, and be honest about turning round at the boulder field if you're behind at 12:30.

Confirm the sailing when you book, and again the week before.

RISK 2

Wind on the Bishop and Clerk summit

Parks says plainly the summit “should be avoided in strong, windy conditions” — unfenced cliffs, sheer drops, and a boulder scramble where wet dolerite gets slick.

Decision rule: check the BOM East Coast forecast Monday night, not on the mountain. If it's blowing, walk to below the boulder field and take the view from there — you lose very little.

Fallback for the day: Fossil Cliffs plus Painted Cliffs plus the convict settlement makes a full, gentler island day.

RISK 3

Mount Amos on wet granite

Not formally gated — which is exactly why it catches people out. The slabs lose grip with the slightest moisture, and the descent is the dangerous direction.

If the rock isn't dry to the touch at the first slab, turn round. Monday has the most slack in it of any day — spend it on a long lunch instead.

RISK 4

Pinnacle Road closed on Wednesday

Mostly a non-event: the standard partial closure is “open to The Springs”, and The Springs is your trailhead. Only a full closure at Fern Tree stops you.

If it is fully closed: Mount Nelson Signal Station — sealed road, big views, café — then a longer Salamanca morning before handover.

What couldn't be confirmed

So you don't act on a number that was guessed rather than found:

  • Which sailings actually load for 15 Sep 2026. The published summer timetable clearly applies, but departures are “subject to demand” and the booking calendar wouldn't render. Your whole Tuesday hinges on the 8:30 — phone 03 6224 8333.
  • Powered-site rates at NRMA Port Arthur, White Beach, BIG4 Iluka, Triabunna, Orford, Barilla and BIG4 Cambridge. None publish them. Triabunna's three directory listings disagree by $19.
  • Tide times are a third-party prediction for Spring Bay, not the official BOM table. The conclusion — that Painted Cliffs doesn't fit Tuesday — holds either way, but cross-check if you want to try.
  • Whether the Spiky Bridge pull-off and the Devil's Corner driveway are sealed. Neither is documented. Look before you turn in.
  • Any length limit on Triabunna marina parking — none published either way, which is why staying at the caravan park and asking to leave the van there is the safer play.
  • Cape Tourville Road's surface — two credible sources each way, and Parks never states it. Treated as unsealed on that basis.
  • Official elevation gain for any walk except Organ Pipes. Parks doesn't publish it.
