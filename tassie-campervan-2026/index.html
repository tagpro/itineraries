Read this first

Four things research turned up that differ from the plan as written.

1 · CRITICAL
Lake Dobson Road is “not suitable for campervans” — Tarn Shelf is at risk
Affects Day 4, the biggest hike of the trip

Parks Tasmania’s own page says the 16 km gravel Lake Dobson Road is not suitable for campervans or buses. Separately, Apollo/DriveNow terms allow unsealed access roads only up to 12 km, and list Tasmania as “permission required” for unsealed travel — driving it without written sign-off can void the whole liability cover, not just cost an excess.

On top of that, snow is a live risk: Parks posted a Lake Dobson Road closure dated 12 September 2025 — the exact calendar date of your Day 1 — and Mount Mawson’s ski tows above Tarn Shelf ran in mid-September in both 2024 and 2025.

Every alpine walk at Mount Field (Tarn Shelf, Pandani Grove, Lake Nicholls, Mt Field East) starts at the top of that road. If it’s shut, the whole alpine half of the park goes with it.

☎ Visitor Centre 03 6288 1149 ☎ Road report 03 6288 1319 See the three workarounds ↓
2 · CRITICAL
Mount Field campground can’t be pre-booked — it’s first come, first served
Affects Night 3 · only 14 powered sites

Parks & Wildlife state plainly: “Bookings are not required. The camp sites are allocated on a first come, first served basis.” Anycamp lists the campground but is not an authorised booking agent — its own page says “No online bookings, please contact via phone.”

If you hold an Anycamp confirmation, verify it directly on 03 6288 1149. PWS’s position is that no reservation exists.

Mitigation: hold a paid booking at Left of Field (5 min away, bookable, free hot showers) as insurance, then try for a $20 powered site at the park on arrival mid-morning — checkout there is 10 am.

3 · HEADS UP
Van must be back by 2:30 pm Wednesday, not 3:00 pm
Affects Day 5 timing

The Apollo Hobart depot (14 Long St, Cambridge) trades 9:00 am – 3:30 pm and requires vehicles back at least one hour before closing. That makes 2:30 pm the real deadline. Day 5 below is built to 2:30 pm with a 30-minute buffer.

Worth also confirming at pickup: written permission for unsealed roads (Tasmania is “permission required”), and whether a Parks Pass is already in the van.

4 · HEADS UP
Parks Pass is $98.35, not $89.50 — and Tasmans Arch is closed
Budget + Day 1 backup plan

The current Holiday Pass (vehicle, up to 2 months) is $98.35; a daily vehicle pass is $49.15. You enter three parks (Tasman, Freycinet, Mount Field), so the Holiday Pass wins from day two. The Annual is $104.75 — only $6 more if you might return within a year. kunanyi / Mt Wellington is free — it isn’t a national park.

Tasmans Arch and Devils Kitchen are closed 15 Apr – 29 Sep 2026 for a $1.9 m upgrade, so they’re out for your whole trip. Waterfall Bay is still walkable, but only from the Waterfall Bay Road end — the Devils Kitchen trailhead is shut.

Good news: Tasmanian school holidays are 3–18 October, so your trip is entirely in term time. No holiday crowds or surcharges.

🧗 Move Mount Amos later, not earlier

Parks: “The track crosses very steep and slippery rock slabs, which are extremely dangerous when wet.” September minimums at Swansea average 5.8 °C — cold enough for heavy overnight dew or frost on bare granite that lingers well past 7:30 am. A dry forecast does not mean dry rock at dawn. Start 09:30–10:00 once the sun has worked on the slabs, or take the easy option. The descent is the dangerous direction.

Alternatives for Day 3 3 options
Skip the morning walk, stop in Richmond instead

If you did Mount Amos on Day 2 or the weather is poor, leave Coles Bay at 08:00 and take the slower route through Richmond — 1823 bridge, bakeries, gaol. Buys you a relaxed arrival at Mount Field and a proper crack at a powered site.

Camp at Left of Field or New Norfolk instead of the park

Both are bookable, which removes the first-come gamble. Left of Field is 5 min from Russell Falls with free hot showers and a bar; New Norfolk is council-run at a confirmed $50 powered, 35 min out but with a supermarket at the door.

Swap Mount Field for the Huon Valley

Tahune Airwalk + Hastings Caves, camping around Geeveston. Removes the Lake Dobson risk entirely and is a shorter drive from Freycinet’s exit. Trade-off: no alpine anything, and the Airwalk carries its own entry fee on top of the parks pass.

kunanyi / Mt Wellington → depot

The Springs · Salamanca · Cambridge

🗺 Open full route in Google Maps 🚦 Pinnacle Road live status
✓ Good news on the road closure risk

Pinnacle Road closes often in September for snow and ice — but the City of Hobart closes it in stages, and the standard partial closure is “open to The Springs while we clear higher up.” The Springs is your trailhead, so the common closure scenario still lets you walk. Only a full closure at Fern Tree stops you. Chains and 4WD don’t help — both are prohibited during closures — but walking up past a closed gate is allowed.

Plan B

Three things can break this trip: Lake Dobson Road, wet granite, and Pinnacle Road. Here’s what to do about each.

RISK 1

Getting to Tarn Shelf without breaking the rental agreement

Ranked best to worst. Sort this before you leave, not on the morning.

1
Get written permission from Apollo at pickup

The only legitimate way to drive it yourself. Ask at the Cambridge counter on Saturday morning and get it in writing on the agreement. Still subject to snow closure on the day.

Cost: $0 · Certainty: medium

2
Tasmanian Wilderness Experiences shuttle to Lake Dobson

Leave the van at the visitor centre. From $95 pp; Hobart departure 7:30 am, Lake Dobson 9:30 am — which fits a Tarn Shelf start, but only just.

⚠ On-demand with a minimum of 4 fares — as a pair you’d pay ~$380 total unless others book onto it. Book well ahead.

Shuttle details →
3
Hire a small car for the day

Cheaper than the shuttle for two people and it solves the vehicle-suitability problem outright. Pick up in New Norfolk or Hobart on the Monday. Still needs the road open.

4
Drop Tarn Shelf, do the Three Falls Circuit and go to kunanyi early

Zero risk and genuinely lovely, but it’s a 2.5-hour day. Fill the afternoon with Richmond, Mount Nelson, or bring the Organ Pipes forward and use Wednesday morning for Salamanca at a walking pace.

There is no lower-altitude substitute — Pandani Grove, Lake Nicholls and every other sub-alpine walk also start from the top of Lake Dobson Road. If it’s shut, the whole alpine half of Mount Field goes with it.

RISK 2

Mount Amos on wet or dewy granite

Not formally gated — it’s your call, which is exactly why it catches people out. The slabs lose grip with the slightest moisture and the descent is the dangerous direction.

Decision rule: if the rock isn’t dry to the touch at the first slab, turn round. Start 09:30–10:00, not 07:30, so the sun has had time on it.

Substitutes that still make a good morning: Wineglass Bay Lookout (2.6 km), Cape Tourville (600 m boardwalk), Sleepy Bay (500 m), Friendly Beaches.

RISK 3

Pinnacle Road closed on Wednesday

Mostly a non-event for you: the standard partial closure is “open to The Springs,” and The Springs is your trailhead. Only a full closure at Fern Tree stops you.

If it is fully closed: Mount Nelson Signal Station (sealed road, big views, café) or the Cascades / Fern Tree area walks. Then a longer Salamanca morning before handover.

What couldn’t be confirmed

So you don’t act on a number that was invented rather than found:

  • September 2026 powered-site rates for NRMA Port Arthur, BIG4 Iluka, White Beach, Swansea, BIG4 Hobart Airport, Discovery Mornington, Barilla, Left of Field and Hobart Showground — all hidden behind dated booking widgets. Every figure for these is a benchmark or an aggregator “from” price.
  • Whether BIG4 Iluka trades in September — nothing suggests a closure, but there’s no explicit confirmation. Ring them.
  • Official elevation gain for any walk except Organ Pipes — Parks Tasmania doesn’t publish it. Third-party figures are indicative.
  • Pinnacle Road closure frequency — no published statistics exist.
  • Whether Apollo specifically names Lake Dobson Road. They don’t; the restriction follows from their 12 km unsealed rule plus Tasmania’s “permission required” status. Confirm at the branch.
  • Snow probability for a specific date — nobody publishes that. “More likely than not” is a judgement from ski-season records and the kunanyi pinnacle temperature series.
