THE SHAPE OF THE WEEK

Why it runs this way round

One rule — no unsealed roads, because Apollo restricts them and a breach can void the liability cover — plus one idea: don't drive the same road twice.

  • ✓Inland on the way up, coast on the way back. The two routes to St Helens are within 15 km and 2 minutes of each other, so you lose nothing — and the Swansea–Bicheno stretch is better southbound, driving toward the Hazards rather than away from them.
  • ✓The B31 means no Hobart backtrack. Turn north out of the depot onto Richmond Rd and you're on the inland route immediately — via Richmond and Colebrook, rejoining the highway below Oatlands.
  • ✓Day 1 is the easiest drive of the week, which matters most on your first day in an unfamiliar 5.94 m van. The Midland Highway is wide, barriered and has the state's best rest area at Campbell Town.
  • ✓Bay of Fires gets a sunset and a sunrise. It faces east — arriving Saturday evening and walking Sunday morning gets both light windows out of one night.
  • ✓Two nights at Coles Bay puts both Freycinet walks on fresh legs, and lets Mount Amos slide to Tuesday if Monday's rock is wet.

5 · MONDAY
Mount Amos goes first because it's the one weather can cancel
Tuesday is its backup

Amos needs dry granite — the slabs lose grip with the slightest moisture. Coles Bay sits in a rain shadow so 70–80% of September mornings are dry, but dew means the rock is only reliably dry by 09:00 about half the time, and nearer 70% by 11:00.

So it takes Monday with a 10:00 start, and the Wineglass circuit takes Tuesday. If Monday is wet, swap them — the circuit is a formed track and copes with weather. Put Amos on Tuesday instead and a wet morning means you simply lose it.

6 · EXPECTATIONS
Bay of Fires is a sunrise, and you'll miss Salamanca Market
Both worth knowing now, not on the day

Bay of Fires faces east. The lichen only really fires with the sun coming off the water at it — sunrise, around 06:11. That's why Sunday starts early.

Salamanca Market is Saturdays only, 08:30–15:00. You're at the depot that morning and Wednesday's stop will be shops and galleries.

Buckland church, and the mill tours: the Callington Mill runs hourly tours 10:00–15:00 at $15 each — a 12:00 tour eats your whole Oatlands stop, so decide in advance whether you want it.

Three things the forecast won't tell you

Daylight isn't a constraint. Just under 12 hours, roughly 06:15 to 18:00. Every walk here fits. Daylight saving doesn't start until October.

A 3.10 m van is a sail. Spring is Tasmania's windiest season. Expect crosswind on the open Midlands plains between Oatlands and Campbell Town, and again on the exposed coast north of Bicheno.

Dusk is the real hazard. Tasmania has the highest per-capita wildlife road toll in the world, most strikes fall between dusk and dawn, and about half involve vehicles over 80 km/h. The Fingal Valley on Saturday and the Buckland hills on Tuesday are both corridors. This plan gets you off the road before dark every night — keep it there.

Turn north out of the depot onto Richmond Rd (B31) — you're on the inland route immediately, no Hobart crossing.

11:35 – 12:25 · STOP 1
Oatlands — Callington Mill

More than 87 Georgian sandstone buildings on one street, the largest such collection in Australia. The mill is an 1837 Lincolnshire tower windmill restored to working order — the only operating mill of its type in the Southern Hemisphere.

RV Friendly Townwide sealed kerbsidetoilets in town
Decide on the mill tour before you arrive. Hourly 10:00–15:00, $15 each, ~40 min with steep internal ladders — a 12:00 tour uses the entire stop. Otherwise walk the Historic Village loop: 30–40 min on town footpaths past the Supreme Court House and gaol wall to the mill precinct. Sealed the whole way.
12:52 – 13:37 · STOP 2 · LUNCH
Ross — the bridge, the bakery, the Female Factory

The best-preserved Georgian village in Tasmania. Ross Bridge (1836) carries 186 panels carved by convict stonemason Daniel Herbert — the third-oldest bridge in Australia and genuinely worth the stop.

  • · Ross Village Bakery, 15 Church St — wood-fired oven, scallop pies. Saturday to ~15:00.
  • · Ross Female Factory, cnr Bond & Portugal — one of only two female convict sites in Tasmania. Weekends 10:00–16:00, free.
  • · Church St is very wide and takes tour coaches daily — 5.94 m is a non-issue.
13:48 – 14:08 · STOP 3 · SERVICES
Campbell Town — shop, fuel, Red Bridge

The easiest stop on the route and the only major rest area on the Midland Highway. The Red Bridge (1836–38, ~1.5 million convict-made bricks) is the oldest brick arch bridge in Australia and the highway drives you over it. The Convict Brick Trail runs along the High St footpath — each brick carved with a convict's name, crime and sentence.

Do the shop here. IGA Everyday, 113 High St, Sat 09:00–17:30 — the last real supermarket before the coast. Fill at Ampol, 85 High St, 24/7. Toilets at Valentine Park, open 24 h.
14:30 → 15:20
Fingal Valley, then down St Marys Pass

The A4 east of Conara is the good driving — the valley narrowing under St Pauls Dome, the South Esk, then a wet-forest plunge to the coast. Optional 10 min at Avoca for St Thomas' (1842). Fifteen minutes at St Marys if you want it.

Take St Marys Pass, not Elephant Pass — the latter is explicitly reported as "not a road for caravans." St Marys Pass is steep with tight corners but it's the main freight route and carries semi-trailers daily. Low gear on the descent. It also has a documented landslip history and live roadworks — check the roadworks link above the week before, and carry 15–20 min of buffer.
15:53 → 18:00
St Helens, then out to the boulders for last light

Check in at Tasman Holiday Parks, 18 St Helens Point Rd — booking #57776, already paid. Check-in from 14:00. Then drive out to Binalong Bay and The Gardens with the evening in hand. Sunset 17:56.

⚠ Reception hours aren't published — ring (03) 6376 2332 from Campbell Town with an ETA if you're behind, since you arrive close to 16:00.

Keep it to the short stuff tonight — The Gardens walk and the Rock Pools are under a kilometre each. The real walk is tomorrow morning. ⚠ Watch the sat-nav: Reids Rd (C849) is unsealed; Binalong Bay Rd (C850) is the sealed one.

09:00 · GO / NO-GO
Two questions over breakfast

Has it been dry for 24 hours, and is the forecast dry? If either answer is no, don't go — the granite slabs lose all grip with the slightest moisture and there is nothing to hold onto.

If it's a no: swap with tomorrow. Do the Wineglass + Hazards circuit today — it's a formed track and copes with weather — and take Amos tomorrow morning instead. That's the whole reason Amos sits on Monday: Tuesday is its backup.
10:00 – 13:00 · MAIN EVENT
Mount Amos, from the Wineglass Bay car park
4.3 km return 2½–3 hrs Grade 4 · Strenuous 397 m ★4.8

The best view of Wineglass Bay there is — higher and cleaner than the lookout, straight down the curve of the beach. It packs more climbing than the 12 km circuit into a third of the distance, and roughly forty minutes of it is hands-on scrambling up bare granite.

10:00, not dawn. Overnight lows of 5–7 °C leave heavy dew and the low September sun burns it off slowly. The rock is reliably dry by 09:00 on perhaps half of September mornings, but nearer 70% by 11:00. The later start is the safer walk, and you have all day.

⚠ Campervans are not permitted in the sand-dune overflow bays at the car park. Mid-September is quiet so the sealed car park should be fine.

Amos is short. Use what's left for Honeymoon Bay and Richardsons Beach — five minutes away on the sealed park road, granite and clear water, and the Hazards go pink at sunset. Or do nothing at all; tomorrow is the longest walk of the week.

08:30 – 13:00 · MAIN EVENT
Wineglass Bay + Hazards Beach circuit
12.1 km loop 4–5 hrs 363 m · Moderate ★4.8 · 3,679 reviews

The most-reviewed walk in eastern Tasmania. Go clockwise: stairs up to the lookout, steps down to the beach, flat across the isthmus, back along Hazards Beach. The crowds thin the moment you pass the lookout, and by the isthmus you'll likely have it to yourselves.

Budget 4–5 hours, not 3½. Parks Tasmania and every guide agree; the shorter figure circulating online is moving time only. An 08:30 start puts you back at the van around 13:00 with the afternoon intact.

Check out of Iluka before you walk — reception opens 08:00, check-out is 10:00, and you won't be back in time otherwise.

14:00 → 15:20
South to Triabunna — and this is the good direction

The Swansea–Bicheno stretch is materially better southbound: you drive toward the Hazards with Great Oyster Bay ahead and to your left, held in the windscreen for 20 km. Going north you'd have had it behind your shoulder. This is why the week runs inland-up, coast-back.

Devil's Corner is an unresolved maybe. Cellar door 10:00–17:00 daily, the lookout tower is free, and the view is the best roadside stop on this coast — but the access road is reported gravel and reviewers note caravanners arriving and leaving over parking. Eyeball it from the highway before turning in, or ring 0448 521 412. Note the Kitchen is closed Tuesdays, so it's a view stop today regardless.

08:50 – 12:20 · MAIN EVENT
Organ Pipes Circuit, from The Springs
9.5 km loop 3–3½ hrs Grade 4 · 486 m free — no pass ★4.7

Up through the Sphinx Rock lookout, traversing beneath the dolerite columns themselves, round via Junction Cabin and The Chalet. Tops out near 1,014 m — high enough for the weather to be its own thing.

This is 9.5 km, not the 6.4 km you may see quoted. That shorter figure belongs to a different track that summits via the Zig Zag at 1,260 m. The Springs circuit is longer, lower, and the right one for a departure day.

Boots, not trail runners — Sphinx Rock to Junction Cabin is rough and rocky, wetter in late winter. Poles help. If three big walks have caught up with you, the Springs–Sphinx Rock–Chalet loop is ~5 km and 2–2½ hours and still gets you the columns.

Plan B

Five ways this wobbles, and what you do instead. None of them ruin a day if you've decided in advance.

RISK 1 · SETTLED

Wednesday’s return, and the $7,500 on your card

The deadline question is closed: Apollo confirmed by email that drop-off can be any time between 9:00 am and 3:00 pm, and that the 9:00 am slot on the booking doesn’t need changing. Wednesday now reaches the depot at 14:20 with 40 minutes spare.

What replaces it as the thing to watch: Drop & Go isn’t available at Hobart, so the van is inspected with you standing there. Cassette and grey tank must already be empty — that’s the Triabunna job on Wednesday morning, and it’s a $250 fee if you skip it.

And the $7,500 bond is debited, not held. Check it comes back after the inspection, and don’t plan on that money for the rest of the trip.

RISK 2

St Marys Pass is closed or slow

It has a documented landslip history and live roadworks were reported on the Esk Main Road — reduced speed limits and occasional lane closures between Avoca, Fingal and St Marys.

Check the roadworks roundup the week before and carry 15–20 minutes of buffer. If the pass is actually shut, the detour is long — back to Campbell Town, east on sealed Lake Leake Road (B34) to Swansea, then north up the coast through Bicheno. About an hour more, and it costs you the Bay of Fires evening. Worth knowing before you commit to Saturday's schedule.

RISK 3

Wet granite on Mount Amos

Roughly a coin-flip by 09:00 in September, better by 11:00. Dew, not rain, is usually the culprit.

This is the one risk the itinerary is actively designed around: swap Monday and Tuesday. Do the Wineglass circuit Monday and take Amos on Tuesday morning at 09:30, reaching Triabunna by 15:00 instead of 15:20. Two nights at Coles Bay is what buys you that option.

RISK 4

Pinnacle Road shut for ice

Snow and ice restrict it April–September, at short notice. The usual gate is at The Springs, which blocks the summit but not your walk, since the circuit starts there. A closure lower down at Fern Tree is the one that hurts.

Swap in Richmond — twelve minutes from the depot and directly on your route in. Or Bonorong for guaranteed devils. Check the status page the night before and again at 06:00.

RISK 5

Saturday runs late

Day 1 has almost no slack. A 10:30 departure arrives 15:53; an 11:00 handover means 16:23, and the St Helens office shuts at 17:00. Any roadworks delay on the pass eats straight into that.

Levers, in order: cut Campbell Town to a 10-minute toilet stop (−10), skip St Marys (−15), drop Ross entirely (−45). Keep Oatlands — it has the working mill and the better village walk. And ring Tasman Holiday Parks on (03) 6376 2332 from Campbell Town if you already know you'll be late.

What couldn't be confirmed

Seven reviewers went over this route across two rounds, and the confirmed bookings have now been folded in. These are what remains unsettled — so you don’t act on a number that was inferred rather than found:

  • Mud Walls Road's surface. A 2017 sealing project completed, but a 2020 account still describes gravel. This is the one that could break Saturday's route. Street View it.
  • Whether the $50.70 Freycinet One-Park pass is per vehicle or per adult. Sources split. It's the difference between saving $48 and saving nothing.
  • St Marys Pass roadworks status for September 2026. Landslip repairs and reduced speed limits were reported; the data is stale. Recheck the week before.
  • Devil's Corner's access road and parking. One review praises the view as worth "the gravel access road"; another notes caravanners arriving and leaving. Eyeball it, or ring 0448 521 412.
  • Maximum vehicle lengths. Rates and availability are now settled — all three parks are booked and confirmed — but none of them publishes a length limit, and none of the three confirmations records the van’s 5.94 m. Mention it when you check in; Iluka’s Premium Slab sites are the surest fit.
  • How often Mount Amos is actually dry. The "about half by 09:00" figure is reasoning from Coles Bay's September climate plus dew, not a published statistic.
  • Live Parks Tasmania track alerts. The alerts register was unreachable from here. Check it the week you travel — and Pinnacle Road on the morning itself.
